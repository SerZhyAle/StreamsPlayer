Broadcast

Start, watch, share and stop what this PC broadcasts: the screen, the camera, the microphone and the system sound. Each source has its own card and starts and stops on its own, so any number of them can be live at once. The top line shows the state of the exchange server, and below it the link-password choice the next broadcast will use. If broadcasting is off, or this PC is signed in to a server only as a guest, the window says so and links to the Broadcasting page of Settings. Closing this window, with Esc too, does not stop a broadcast: the Broadcast button in the main window stays red while anything is live and opens this window again.

In this order

  1. If the window says broadcasting is off, press Settings.. and allow it on the Broadcasting page.
  2. On the card of what you want to send, choose the device if the card offers a choice.
  3. Press the start button of the card and wait for it to read live. Cancel ends a start that is still waiting.
  4. Read on the card where it went: the local network address, Google Drive.
  5. Hand it out with Share or send it to one of your own devices.
  6. Press Stop on the card, or Stop all, to end it.

The elements

Exchange serverShows how the connection to your exchange server is doing; opens its settings.

The line beside this icon says whether this PC is connected to the exchange server of your account. A broadcast announced on the server reaches your other devices only while the connection is up. When the server cannot be reached and a broadcast needs it, the line says so, and the Broadcast button in the main window carries a small warning mark.

Pressing the icon opens the Exchange server page in Settings, where the account is set up.

Next
Settings opens on the Exchange server page, on top of this window.
PC screenBroadcasts one whole monitor, with or without a sound.

Choose the monitor and the one sound that travels with the screen: none, the microphone or the system sound. The choices are read when the broadcast starts, so they are unavailable while it is live. The whole monitor is sent, including notifications and passwords, and content that Windows protects may come out blank.

The card is not shown on a PC whose Windows has no H.264 encoder; the window says so instead.

Why
To show what is on this PC to your phone or another PC.
Next
The card reads starting, then live since the time it went on the air. A refused start shows its reason on the card and leaves the source off.
Undo
Press Stop on the card.
PC cameraBroadcasts one camera, with or without a sound.

Choose the camera and the one sound that travels with it, as for the screen. If Windows denies the camera to this application, or another program holds it, the broadcast does not start and the card says which.

Next
The card reads starting, then live since the time it went on the air.
Undo
Press Stop on the card.
PC microphoneBroadcasts the microphone you choose.

Windows default follows whatever Windows uses as the default recording device; the other entries are the microphones Windows lists. The list is read only after broadcasting is allowed, so no capture device is touched before that. Mono or stereo, the sample rate and the bitrate are set in Settings.

Next
The card reads starting, then live since the time it went on the air.
Undo
Press Stop on the card.
PC system soundBroadcasts the sound this PC plays.

Works like the microphone, with the sound this PC plays as the source. On a Windows that cannot leave this application's own sound out, the card says that a broadcast you play here would be sent on again.

Next
The card reads starting, then live since the time it went on the air.
Undo
Press Stop on the card.
ShareHands a live source to other people or devices, and says where it went.

A live card shows where its source went: the local network address when Local network is on in Settings, Google Drive when it is used, and how many receivers are connected directly to this PC.

Share copies the link, copies its password, shows the link as a QR code for a phone, or saves the description file. The password is never inside the link; with the choice No password there is nothing to copy. Cast to your own device offers the source to one of your devices that is online and can play it; the device asks first. Show addressed devices names the devices the announcement was sealed to. It is the list of addressed devices, not of people watching.

Undo
Nothing here changes the broadcast. Changing the link password in Settings stops the previous one at once.
Stop allEnds every broadcast running from this PC.

Stops all the sources that are live; each card goes back to off. The button is available only while something is running. A single source is stopped with Stop on its own card.

Next
The sources stop at once and the Broadcast button in the main window returns to its normal colour. Start any of them again from its card.
Settings..Opens the Broadcasting page of Settings.

Broadcasting stays a setting in one place: the permission, starting the chosen sources with the application, the local network and its port, the listener cap, the quality and the link password. This window shows the current link-password choice and links there.

Next
Settings opens on the Broadcasting page, on top of this window.
HelpOpens this page.

Every window that has help carries this button near its top-right corner. It opens the page of the window you are in, in your web browser; the application stays open.

Next
The page opens in your browser. Nothing in the application changes.
Shortcut
F1 does the same from anywhere in the window.
