@@what-title
What it is & why

@@what-p1
STREAMS Player is an independent Windows desktop app for internet radio, live video, and RTSP network cameras. It reads a curated, public stream catalog so you get a working list of channels on first run - then lets you search, sort, pin, and add your own, all stored locally on your machine.

@@what-p2
The point is calm: a player that stays a player. The catalog refreshes only when you ask, your manually added and imported streams are never overwritten by an update, and no account is required and there is no advertising, analytics, or author-run service. What runs on your PC is what you started.

@@features-title
What it can do

@@features-description
Everything needed to find, keep, and play a stream - grouped by what you are actually doing.

@@cap-find-title
Find a channel

@@cap-find-1
A curated catalog, plus a built-in copy of it you can apply on first launch with no network. A channel the catalog gave no icon still gets a face of its own - initials and a colour taken from its name, plus its country code where known.

@@cap-find-2
Filter by category, topic, language, country, media type - open the row when you need it.

@@cap-find-3
Search always sits on the title line.

@@cap-find-4
Pin the channels you return to.

@@cap-find-5
Play a random radio station from the whole catalog - one that stays silent is replaced by the next draw.

@@cap-play-title
Play the right way

@@cap-play-1
Radio plays in the main window and names the track on air whenever the station provides it.

@@cap-play-2
Stop the sound without losing the station - the volume you set and the sleep timer stay, ready to resume.

@@cap-play-3
Live video and RTSP open in a focused player window that shows the track on air over the picture.

@@cap-play-4
A ~15-second live buffer, and video that settles at the quality your connection holds, then opens there next time.

@@cap-play-5
Record the live video or radio you are playing straight to a file, without interrupting playback: video goes to the Recordings folder in Videos, radio to the Recordings folder in Music, or to folders you choose.

@@cap-play-6
Listen to a live audio or video broadcast from a FastMediaSorter phone or watch, directly over your local network - no server in between.

@@cap-window-2

@@cap-window-3
Very Small to Large stream tiles, a light, dark or Windows-matching theme, and a Settings window with search and collapsible groups that reopens where you left it.

@@cap-window-5
The player says why over the video whenever the picture stops - and re-opens a stream that froze silently.

@@cap-window-6
Shrink the catalog to a small captionless panel while radio plays - the station and track on top, and below them the volume, the sleep timer, previous and next station, a random station, Record, and the panel's own always-on-top, back and minimize buttons.

@@use-title
How to use it

@@use-description
Open it and pick a channel - that is the whole flow. Install it with the setup executable, from the Microsoft Store, with winget, or unpack the portable ZIP - or run it from source; you can also launch a stream directly from the command line.

@@use-1-title
Run it from source

@@use-1-text
With the .NET 10 SDK installed, restore, build, and launch from the repository root.

@@use-2-title
Pick and play

@@use-2-text
Browse the catalog, filter or search, then select a channel. Radio starts in the main window; video and RTSP open in the player window. An ordinary launch starts nothing; turn on Resume playback on startup in [[glyph:app.settings]] Settings and it brings back whatever was playing when you last closed the app.

@@use-3-title
Open any URL directly

@@use-3-text
Play a stream without touching the catalog.

@@use-4-title
Save a channel to a shortcut

@@use-4-text
Open a saved channel's [[glyph:nav.more]] three-dot menu and use Copy launch command or Create desktop shortcut. Both use the channel's stored id.

@@use-5-title
Report a problem with the logs

@@use-5-text
Open Settings from the gear button in the header (or the [[glyph:nav.more]] Operations menu) and press Send logs to the author on the About page. The app packs the diagnostic logs of the last ten launches with a short summary of your version, Windows and settings into the Saved files folder (Downloads by default), then opens your mail program with the message ready. The confirmation shows the complete path and can open that folder when you ask; you attach the archive and press Send.

@@privacy-eyebrow
STREAMS Player · Privacy

@@privacy-title
Private by default.

@@privacy-intro
No account is required for ordinary playback. The optional exchange account belongs to your own server. There is no advertising, analytics, telemetry or service operated by the author.

@@privacy-network-title
Network access

@@privacy-network
The application connects only on your action, and only to the place that action needs: the public stream catalog and the optional preview pack offered after it, downloaded from GitHub when you import the catalog or accept the pack; the provider of a stream you play, record or open About channel for, or whose video preview refreshes while Grid mode is visible; a FastMediaSorter phone or watch you imported, reached directly on your local network; and, only if you ask for them in Settings on the Playback page, the optional FFmpeg libraries for the experimental FlyleafLib engine, downloaded from a third-party GitHub project (BtbN/FFmpeg-Builds). Each request goes straight to that destination and is subject to its policies; none passes through a service run by the author. A TV schedule is downloaded only from the address you enter yourself in Settings on the Library page, once each time you press Download schedule, and never in the background. The optional Exchange source in Library settings connects to your own server over TLS. Enrollment sends your login, password or pairing code, device identity, product version and receiver capabilities. Later connections use the device identity and device token. It holds one control connection with keepalives and reconnects while enabled; disabling it closes the connection. Every certificate leaf needs your explicit approval, including after renewal. 

@@privacy-site-title
This website

@@privacy-site
This section is about this website, not the application. Every page loads its typefaces from Google Fonts: your browser requests a stylesheet from fonts.googleapis.com and the font files from fonts.gstatic.com, so Google receives your IP address, your browser details and the address of the page you are reading. The home page also asks api.github.com once for the latest release, so that the installer button points at the current file; GitHub receives your IP address and your browser details. The pages themselves are served by GitHub Pages (serzhyale.github.io). The site has no analytics, advertising or tracking, sets no cookies, and keeps only your theme and language choice in your browser's local storage. Each of these services is subject to its own policy; none is operated by the author.
@@privacy-local-title
Local data

@@privacy-local
STREAMS Player stores its catalog state, your added and imported channels, pins, collections, hidden channels, listening history, playback marks, cached preview frames within a 150 MB budget, the optional FFmpeg libraries if you downloaded them, and the diagnostic logs of the last ten launches under [[appdata]]. Recordings and saved frames go to the folders you choose for them, or by default to Recordings in Videos, Recordings in Music and Frames in Pictures. This data stays on your device unless you copy or back it up - or unless you use Send logs to the author in Settings on the About page, which packs those diagnostic logs plus your app version, Windows version and settings into an archive and opens your mail program so you can attach and send it to [[email]]. That archive is prepared only when you press that button, the logs in it name the streams that were played, and nothing is ever sent without you pressing Send in your own mail program. A TV schedule you downloaded and your schedule channel choices are kept there too; Remove schedule in Settings deletes both. The password and pairing code are discarded. The account, token and certificate pin are protected for your Windows user in a separate file; removing the account forgets them. Diagnostic bundles omit that file and redact account values. Exchange connections begin only after your explicit source setup.

@@trust-title
Windows warned you. Here is what it means.

@@trust-intro
STREAMS Player is a free app from an independent developer. If Windows stopped it the first time you opened the download, this page says what that screen is, why it appears, exactly what to click, and what the app never does. It takes about thirty seconds to read.

@@trust-what-title
“Windows protected your PC”

@@trust-what-1
This screen is Microsoft Defender SmartScreen. Under the heading it says it “prevented an unrecognized app from starting”, and after More info it names the publisher as “Unknown publisher”. It appears when you open a file downloaded from the internet that is not digitally signed and that Windows has not yet seen run on many PCs.

@@trust-what-2
It is a caution about a file Windows does not know yet, not a report of something found inside it - and it is Windows doing its job: it cannot tell who made an unsigned file, so it asks you before running it.

@@trust-why-title
Why it appears for this app

@@trust-why-1
The installer, the portable ZIP and the winget package of STREAMS Player are not code-signed. A code-signing certificate costs hundreds of dollars a year, and for a free app with no income we chose not to buy one for now. That is a decision, not an oversight - and for an unsigned file, a warning is exactly what Windows should show.

@@trust-why-2
SmartScreen also weighs reputation: the more people run the same file without trouble, the sooner Windows recognizes it and the warning stops. Every release is a new file that starts from zero, which is why the warning can come back after an update and why it fades as a release is used.

@@trust-why-3
The Microsoft Store version is signed by Microsoft and does not show this screen. Every GitHub release also publishes a .sha256 file beside each download: run [[hash]] in PowerShell on the file you downloaded, and the result should match the value in that file.

@@trust-click-title
What to click

@@trust-click-intro
On the “Windows protected your PC” screen:

@@trust-click-1
Click More info, under the message.

@@trust-click-2
Check that the file name is the STREAMS Player file you downloaded, then click Run anyway.

@@trust-click-3
That is all - the installer, or the app from the ZIP, starts normally. You never need to switch SmartScreen off or disable your antivirus, and the app itself never needs administrator rights.

@@trust-admin-title
“Do you want to allow this app from an unknown publisher to make changes to your device?”

@@trust-admin-1
You should not normally see this prompt. By default the installer installs for you alone, into [[installdir]], and needs no administrator rights for that. If it asks how to install, choose Install for me only (recommended).

@@trust-admin-2
Windows shows this prompt only if you choose Install for all users. The installer then uses administrator rights for exactly three things:

@@trust-admin-item-1
copying the program into the Program Files folder, so every account on this PC can run it;

@@trust-admin-item-2
adding STREAMS Player to the Start menu of every account, and to the shared desktop if you ticked that option;

@@trust-admin-item-3
registering it with Windows as an installed app, so it can be removed later.

@@trust-admin-3
One action removes all three together: Settings > Apps > Installed apps (Apps & features on Windows 10) > STREAMS Player > Uninstall. If you would rather not give administrator rights, click No and run the installer again with Install for me only.

@@trust-av-title
If your antivirus holds the file back

@@trust-av
Some antivirus programs are cautious with new, unsigned files. If yours quarantines STREAMS Player, restore it and add an exception for its folder only - [[installdir]] for the installer, or the folder you unpacked the ZIP into. An exception for that one folder is all it takes, and you can remove it at any time; do not turn your antivirus off.

@@trust-never-title
What STREAMS Player never does

@@trust-never-1
It never needs administrator rights to install or run, never adds itself to Windows startup, and installs no background service.

@@trust-never-2
No account is required for ordinary playback. The optional exchange account belongs to your own server. There is no advertising, analytics, telemetry or service operated by the author.

@@trust-never-3
It connects only when you act, and only to what that action needs: the public stream catalog and its optional preview pack on GitHub, the provider of a stream you play, record or preview, a FastMediaSorter phone or watch on your own network, and the optional FFmpeg libraries if you ask for them in Settings. A TV schedule is fetched only from an address you enter yourself, and only when you press Download schedule.
@@trust-never-4
It never downloads the catalog on its own - a refresh happens only when you ask for it.

@@trust-never-5
It never sends your data or logs anywhere by itself. Send logs to the author on the About page in Settings only prepares an archive and a message in your own mail program; nothing leaves until you press Send there.
@@trust-never-6
Uninstalling it never deletes your channels, pins or history - they stay in [[appdata]] until you remove that folder yourself.
