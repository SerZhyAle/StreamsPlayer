@@what-title
What it is & why

@@what-p1
STREAMS Player is an independent Windows desktop app for internet radio, live video, and RTSP network cameras. It reads a curated, public stream catalog so you get a working list of channels on first run - then lets you search, sort, pin, and add your own, all stored locally on your machine.

@@what-p2
The point is calm: a player that stays a player. The catalog refreshes only when you ask, your manually added and imported streams are never overwritten by an update, and no account is required and there is no advertising, analytics, or author-run service. What runs on your PC is what you started.

@@use-title
How to use it

@@use-description
Open it and pick a channel - that is the whole flow. Install it with the setup executable, from the Microsoft Store, with winget, or unpack the portable ZIP - or run it from source; you can also launch a stream directly from the command line.

@@use-1-title
Run it from source

@@use-1-text
With the .NET 10 SDK installed, restore, build, and launch from the repository root.

@@use-2-title
Pick and play

@@use-2-text
Browse the catalog, filter or search, then select a channel. Radio starts in the main window; video and RTSP open in the player window. An ordinary launch starts nothing; turn on Resume playback on startup in [[glyph:app.settings]] Settings and it brings back whatever was playing when you last closed the app.

In the 64-bit viewer, right-click a video or audio file or its folder and choose Broadcast. Share Manager exports a .spres file, QR code or quick link for import in StreamsPlayer. A folder is one playlist channel with a frozen list; Refresh replaces that list and Revoke stops its link. The HTTP gateway has its own switch, off by default. Each token grants read-only access to one broadcast and normally expires after 24 hours. HTTP is unencrypted: share the document privately and keep internet access off unless you need it.

@@privacy-title
Private by default.

@@privacy-intro
No account is required for ordinary playback. The optional exchange account belongs to a private server - your own, or one run by someone who invited you - never to a service of this product. There is no advertising, analytics, telemetry or service operated by the author.

@@privacy-network-title
Network access

@@privacy-network
The application connects only on your action, and only to the place that action needs: the public stream catalog and the optional preview pack offered after it, downloaded from GitHub when you import the catalog or accept the pack; the provider of a stream you play, record or open About channel for, or whose video preview refreshes while Grid mode is visible; a FastMediaSorter phone or watch you imported, reached directly on your local network; and, only if you ask for them in Settings on the Playback page, the optional FFmpeg libraries for the experimental FlyleafLib engine, downloaded from a third-party GitHub project (BtbN/FFmpeg-Builds). Each request goes straight to that destination and is subject to its policies; none passes through a service run by the author. A TV schedule is downloaded only from the address you enter yourself in Settings on the Library page, once each time you press Download schedule, and never in the background. The optional Exchange source in Library settings connects to your own server over TLS. Signing in sends your login, password or pairing code, device identity, product version and receiver capabilities; this program signs in only and never creates an account. Later connections use the device identity and device token. It holds one control connection with keepalives and reconnects while enabled; disabling it closes the connection. Import a connection string, a file or a QR picture in Library settings, or paste or drop one in the main window. A connection string carries the server address and an optional certificate fingerprint, with no secret; an invitation inside it is checked and dropped, never sent or kept. Reading it only fills the form. The server's address and port are under Details in the form: check them before entering your password or pairing code; neither is kept. A supplied fingerprint must match, with no trust prompt. Without one, you approve the server fingerprint before signing in; a certificate renewed with the same key is accepted, and any other change needs your approval. Changing the account's password sends the old and the new password to your server once and keeps neither. No local agent or second program is needed.  When a broadcast plays through your exchange server with a pinned certificate, that one stream is forwarded by a temporary local proxy on 127.0.0.1 which exists only while the broadcast plays and is reachable only from this device. Broadcasting: the application can send four sources of this PC live - a microphone you choose, the sound the PC is playing, one whole monitor you choose and a camera you choose, the screen and the camera each optionally carrying one sound (the microphone or the system sound) in the same broadcast - to the exchange server of your own account or over your local network, only when you allow it in Settings and start each source yourself; it is off until then, nothing is captured while it is off, and a visible indicator with a stop is shown while a source is live, naming the monitor or camera that is live. Video is H.264 up to 1280x720 at 30 frames per second, about 2 Mbit/s, made with Windows’ own encoder; no component is added. Everything on the chosen monitor is sent, including notifications and passwords, and content that Windows protects may come out blank. A PC signed in to a server only as a guest does not broadcast. Listeners connect over your local network, if you enable it, or through your exchange server. You can limit the number of listeners. Each listener on the local network and each tunnel uses this PC’s upload; through the server’s relay this PC sends one copy for all listeners. A broadcast link, file or QR code you hand out carries the title, addresses and keys that open the broadcast, and never the password. In Settings you choose how the password is made: a random password for each broadcast (the default), one you set (1 to 64 characters, kept only in the Windows secret store of your Windows account), or none, in which case anyone who gets the link can watch and listen. A change while you broadcast revokes the previous password at once. The Share menu of the live indicator copies the link, copies the password, shows the link as a QR code for a phone to scan and saves the description file. Treat the link as a secret and share it only with people you choose. The account directory carries those keys in an encrypted box.

In the 64-bit viewer, right-click a video or audio file or its folder and choose Broadcast. Share Manager exports a .spres file, QR code or quick link for import in StreamsPlayer. A folder is one playlist channel with a frozen list; Refresh replaces that list and Revoke stops its link. The HTTP gateway has its own switch, off by default. Each token grants read-only access to one broadcast and normally expires after 24 hours. HTTP is unencrypted: share the document privately and keep internet access off unless you need it.


@@privacy-exchange-title
Exchange server, broadcasting and encryption

@@privacy-exchange-private
An exchange server is private: everyone runs their own, or uses one whose owner invited them. There is no public exchange server, and no exchange server is a service of this product.

@@privacy-exchange-honest
The exchange server connects your devices, so it knows the names of your account, devices, folders and broadcasts, sees which devices are online, and how much is sent and when. It cannot open what goes through it: folders and their files, broadcasts and the clipboard are encrypted on your device and opened only on your other devices or by the people you gave access to. To look inside, whoever runs the server would have to add a device of their own to your account, and it would appear in your list of devices.

@@privacy-exchange-sends
To sign in, this PC sends your login and your password or pairing code once, then its device identity, its device name (STREAMS Player, not the name of your PC), product, version, platform (Windows) and roles, its receiver capabilities and the public half of its encryption key. While the connection is on, the server sees that this device is online. When you broadcast, it receives the broadcast's title (for the screen and the camera it names the monitor or the camera), the fact that it is audio or video, and the audio or video itself, encrypted on this PC before it leaves; the private key never leaves this PC.

@@privacy-exchange-broadcast
Broadcasting can send four sources of this PC: a microphone you choose, the sound the PC is playing, one whole monitor you choose and a camera you choose; the screen and the camera can each carry one sound, the microphone or the system sound, in the same broadcast. Video is H.264 up to 1280x720 at 30 frames per second, about 2 Mbit/s, made with Windows' own encoder; no component is added. It is off until you allow it in Settings, nothing is captured while it is off, and you start each source yourself; a visible indicator with a stop is shown while a source is live and names the monitor or camera that is live. Everything on the chosen monitor is sent, including notifications and passwords, and content that Windows protects may come out blank. A PC signed in to a server only as a guest does not broadcast. Each listener on the local network and each tunnel uses this PC's upload; through the server's relay this PC sends one copy for all listeners. The link, the file and the QR code you hand to someone outside the account never contain the password. You choose how it is made: a random password for each broadcast (the default), one you set (1 to 64 characters, kept only in the Windows secret store of your Windows account), or none, in which case anyone who gets the link can watch and listen. A change while you broadcast revokes the previous password at once. The Share menu of the live indicator copies the link and the password, shows the link as a QR code for a phone to scan, and saves the description file. Only if you also turn on the option to start with the application do the sources you left chosen start again when the application starts, once the account is online. A listener's device can record what it receives, and this program cannot prevent that.

@@privacy-exchange-camera
The camera of this PC is used in two ways, each for one thing only. The first is reading a connection QR when you press "Scan with the camera" in Settings: while the scan window is open, the picture is looked at in memory to find the QR; it is never stored, sent or logged, and the camera is released the moment the window closes. Where Windows reports no camera, that button is not offered. The second is sending the picture of the camera you choose as a broadcast, and only when you start it yourself with "Broadcast camera" in Settings: the picture is then encoded on this PC and sent as that broadcast, and the camera is released when the broadcast stops.

@@privacy-exchange-account
This program signs in and never creates an account. An account is deleted in FastMediaSorter on the phone, or by asking whoever runs the server; the exchange portal says how. Signing out on this device removes this device, not the account.

@@privacy-site-title
This website

@@privacy-site
This section is about this website, not the application. Every page loads its typefaces from Google Fonts: your browser requests a stylesheet from fonts.googleapis.com and the font files from fonts.gstatic.com, so Google receives your IP address, your browser details and the address of the page you are reading. The home page also asks api.github.com once for the latest release, so that the installer button points at the current file; GitHub receives your IP address and your browser details. The pages themselves are served by GitHub Pages (serzhyale.github.io). The site has no analytics, advertising or tracking, sets no cookies, and keeps only your theme and language choice in your browser's local storage. Each of these services is subject to its own policy; none is operated by the author.
@@privacy-local-title
Local data

@@privacy-local
STREAMS Player stores its catalog state, your added and imported channels, pins, collections, hidden channels, listening history, playback marks, cached preview frames within a 150 MB budget, the optional FFmpeg libraries if you downloaded them, and the diagnostic logs of the last ten launches under [[appdata]]. Recordings and saved frames go to the folders you choose for them, or by default to Recordings in Videos, Recordings in Music and Frames in Pictures. This data stays on your device unless you copy or back it up - or unless you use Send logs to the author in Settings on the About page, which packs those diagnostic logs plus your app version, Windows version and settings into an archive and opens your mail program so you can attach and send it to [[email]]. That archive is prepared only when you press that button, the logs in it name the streams that were played, and nothing is ever sent without you pressing Send in your own mail program. A TV schedule you downloaded and your schedule channel choices are kept there too; Remove schedule in Settings deletes both. The password and pairing code are discarded. The account, tokens and certificate pins are protected for your Windows user in separate files; signing out on this device forgets them. Diagnostic bundles omit that file and redact account values. Exchange connections begin only after your explicit source setup. This device keeps one exchange account of its own and a limited number of guest accounts beside it (Settings states the number); a guest account sees only what was given to it and announces nothing on its server. Signing in to a different own account asks first, signs the new account in, and only then leaves the current one; signing in again to the same account keeps the device identity. Signing out on this device asks the server to remove this device, then forgets the local connection even if the server is unavailable. Imported channels stay. If the server could not be notified, the app says the device may remain listed until another device removes it; it never retries in the background. When the account's password is changed elsewhere, this device is signed out, keeps the server and login, and asks for the new password; nothing retries.

@@trust-title
Windows warned you. Here is what it means.

@@trust-intro
STREAMS Player is a free app from an independent developer. If Windows stopped it the first time you opened the download, this page says what that screen is, why it appears, exactly what to click, and what the app never does. It takes about thirty seconds to read.

@@trust-what-title
“Windows protected your PC”

@@trust-what-1
This screen is Microsoft Defender SmartScreen. Under the heading it says it “prevented an unrecognized app from starting”, and after More info it names the publisher as “Unknown publisher”. It appears when you open a file downloaded from the internet that is not digitally signed and that Windows has not yet seen run on many PCs.

@@trust-what-2
It is a caution about a file Windows does not know yet, not a report of something found inside it - and it is Windows doing its job: it cannot tell who made an unsigned file, so it asks you before running it.

@@trust-why-title
Why it appears for this app

@@trust-why-1
The installer, the portable ZIP and the winget package of STREAMS Player are not code-signed. A code-signing certificate costs hundreds of dollars a year, and for a free app with no income we chose not to buy one for now. That is a decision, not an oversight - and for an unsigned file, a warning is exactly what Windows should show.

@@trust-why-2
SmartScreen also weighs reputation: the more people run the same file without trouble, the sooner Windows recognizes it and the warning stops. Every release is a new file that starts from zero, which is why the warning can come back after an update and why it fades as a release is used.

@@trust-why-3
The Microsoft Store version is signed by Microsoft and does not show this screen. Every GitHub release also publishes a .sha256 file beside each download: run [[hash]] in PowerShell on the file you downloaded, and the result should match the value in that file.

@@trust-click-title
What to click

@@trust-click-intro
On the “Windows protected your PC” screen:

@@trust-click-1
Click More info, under the message.

@@trust-click-2
Check that the file name is the STREAMS Player file you downloaded, then click Run anyway.

@@trust-click-3
That is all - the installer, or the app from the ZIP, starts normally. You never need to switch SmartScreen off or disable your antivirus, and the app itself never needs administrator rights.

@@trust-admin-title
“Do you want to allow this app from an unknown publisher to make changes to your device?”

@@trust-admin-1
You should not normally see this prompt. By default the installer installs for you alone, into [[installdir]], and needs no administrator rights for that. If it asks how to install, choose Install for me only (recommended).

@@trust-admin-2
Windows shows this prompt only if you choose Install for all users. The installer then uses administrator rights for exactly three things:

@@trust-admin-item-1
copying the program into the Program Files folder, so every account on this PC can run it;

@@trust-admin-item-2
adding STREAMS Player to the Start menu of every account, and to the shared desktop if you ticked that option;

@@trust-admin-item-3
registering it with Windows as an installed app, so it can be removed later.

@@trust-admin-3
One action removes all three together: Settings > Apps > Installed apps (Apps & features on Windows 10) > STREAMS Player > Uninstall. If you would rather not give administrator rights, click No and run the installer again with Install for me only.

@@trust-av-title
If your antivirus holds the file back

@@trust-av
Some antivirus programs are cautious with new, unsigned files. If yours quarantines STREAMS Player, restore it and add an exception for its folder only - [[installdir]] for the installer, or the folder you unpacked the ZIP into. An exception for that one folder is all it takes, and you can remove it at any time; do not turn your antivirus off.

@@trust-never-title
What STREAMS Player never does

@@trust-never-1
It never needs administrator rights to install or run, never adds itself to Windows startup, and installs no background service.

@@trust-never-2
No account is required for ordinary playback. The optional exchange account belongs to a private server - your own, or one run by someone who invited you - never to a service of this product. There is no advertising, analytics, telemetry or service operated by the author.

@@trust-never-3
It connects only when you act, and only to what that action needs: the public stream catalog and its optional preview pack on GitHub, the provider of a stream you play, record or preview, a FastMediaSorter phone or watch on your own network, and the optional FFmpeg libraries if you ask for them in Settings. A TV schedule is fetched only from an address you enter yourself, and only when you press Download schedule.
@@trust-never-4
It never downloads the catalog on its own - a refresh happens only when you ask for it.

@@trust-never-5
It never sends your data or logs anywhere by itself. Send logs to the author on the About page in Settings only prepares an archive and a message in your own mail program; nothing leaves until you press Send there.
@@trust-never-6
Uninstalling it never deletes your channels, pins or history - they stay in [[appdata]] until you remove that folder yourself.

@@support-stream-title
A channel stops playing

@@support-stream
The catalog is a public list of streams that other people run: radio stations, broadcasters, camera owners. STREAMS Player keeps their addresses on your device and, as the privacy page says, connects to the network only when you act; the streams themselves stay with whoever runs them, so a stream can move, change format, become available only in another region or go off the air for good, and the catalog changes over time. When a channel fails, the Stream unavailable window may offer Retry, Copy report, Hide (Delete, for a channel you added or imported yourself) and Keep. Which of them appear depends on the failure: Retry is left out for an address the app cannot play at all, and Hide or Delete is left out when there is no network connection, because the channel was then never reached. Retry tries again, or you can simply open another channel; Hide takes the channel out of your list, and you can bring it back: press Hidden on the Library page in Settings, then Unhide next to the channel; Copy report puts a short note on your clipboard to paste into an issue, and the app sends nothing. Import channels from the internet, in the Operations menu, downloads the current catalog, and only when you ask. A stream that is gone is not a fault in the app, and no fix is promised.

@@support-warning-title
Windows warned you

@@privacy-drive
Google sign-in is optional and requests only drive.appdata. Google receives the sign-in and API requests at accounts.google.com, oauth2.googleapis.com and www.googleapis.com. Only device names (fmsx-device-*) and broadcast announcements (fmsx-broadcast-*) in your hidden application data are read. This PC writes and renews its own announcements while broadcasting and removes them at the end; media never passes through Drive. The directory is polled only while its window is open and not minimized. The refresh token is protected for your Windows user. Signing out forgets it and asks Google to revoke access; a failed revocation is reported. Announcements stay in your Google account without additional end-to-end encryption.
